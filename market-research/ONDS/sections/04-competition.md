**Read:** C-UAS is ONDS's **highest-conviction market** (~25% CAGR), and its **dual modality** (Iron Drone kinetic net-capture + Sentrycs non-jamming protocol takeover) is differentiated and usable in regulated/populated airspace. **But** the field is crowded with far better-capitalized players — Anduril ($61B, ~$2.2B revenue) above all, and a now-profitable DroneShield (A$216M revenue) as the scaled public pure-play — and procurement increasingly favors scaled lead-system-integrators (e.g., Perennial's $500M IDIQ).

### 4.3 Drone inspection (vs Cyberhawk, pending close Q3 2026)
Competitors: **Percepto** (AIM), **Flyability** (confined-space), Sky-Futures, Nearmap, plus traditional inspection firms. Cyberhawk's edge is the **iHawk software/data layer** (digital twins, 500k+ assets, ~95% recurring) — a higher-margin position than hardware-centric inspection rivals. Headroom is large (Cyberhawk ~0.1% share of a ~$33B market) but it's a small incumbent.

### 4.4 Critical-infrastructure / rail private wireless (vs Ondas Networks FullMAX / 802.16t)
*Figures verified 2026-06-19. No standalone rail-segment revenue is publicly broken out for these large private/divisional units — figures intentionally omitted rather than fabricated.*
| Company | Role vs Ondas | Verified positioning |
|---|---|---|
| **Siemens Mobility** | **PARTNER** (not a head-to-head competitor) | Exclusive partnership to sell Siemens-branded "Airlink" radios built on Ondas' 802.16s/t SDR to North American rail; joint Head-of-Train, Amtrak **ACSES 220 MHz** (~$2.8M), and European on-loco radio work. *Latent* systems-level competitor only. |
| **Meteorcomm LLC** | **Incumbent competitor** | The rail industry's interoperable PTC/ITC messaging provider — operates **ITCnet®** (220 MHz). Owned 25% each by Class I affiliates (BNSF Communications, CSX, NS Spectrum, Ekanet/Union Pacific). |
| **Nokia** | Competitor (adjacent market) | Global rail-comms leader; lead **FRMCS** (5G GSM-R successor) vendor; deployed world-first 1900 MHz 5G SA with Deutsche Bahn. |
| **Ericsson** | Competitor (adjacent market) | Active FRMCS 5G vendor; on the ETSI Railway Communications group defining FRMCS. |

**Clarifications:** FRMCS (Nokia/Ericsson, 5G, European/global, GSM-R successor) and AAR **802.16t** (North American freight) are **distinct, non-overlapping markets** — Nokia/Ericsson compete with Ondas mainly in the *broader* critical-infrastructure private-wireless category and *non-US* rail, not the AAR dot-16 freight buildout. The **legacy 900 MHz ATCS network is being retired** (FCC-driven, ~Sept 14 2025 deadline; ~$110M industry investment), opening the 900 MHz "A-block" where Ondas did its first 802.16t deployment (Chicago).

**Read:** Ondas's moat here is real (patented FullMAX embedded in **IEEE 802.16t**, AAR standard selection, exclusive Siemens channel, software-upgradable installed base) — but this segment is now **deconsolidated** and ~2% of revenue, so it's optionality rather than a core competitive front today.

### 4.5 Where ONDS wins / where it's vulnerable
**Wins:**
- **Regulatory/certification moat** — first FAA Type Certification (Optimus 1-EX) + first automated-BVLOS-without-operator approval; hard and slow to replicate.
- **Standards-embedded networking IP** — FullMAX inside IEEE 802.16t with AAR/rail lock-in.
- **Breadth / "system of systems"** — air + ground + C-UAS + stratospheric + AI software via 2026 M&A, plus a Palantir tie-in and prime-contractor access (Mistral).
- **Dual-modality C-UAS** usable where jamming is illegal.
- **Emerging data moat** — Cyberhawk's recurring iHawk platform (pending).

**Vulnerable:**
- **Outgunned on capital/scale** by Anduril ($61B), AVAV+BlueHalo (>$1.7B rev), Axon-backed Dedrone.
- **Moat increasingly assembled by acquisition**, not organic IP — integration risk.
- **Procurement favors scaled LSIs** over sub-scale point-solution vendors.
- **No clean kinetic-interceptor TAM** — Iron Drone's exact niche is unsized and contested (Anduril Roadrunner).
- **Capital-structure fragility** — serial dilution funds the competitive push; a risk-off turn closes that window.
