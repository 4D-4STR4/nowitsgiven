# Market Research — Steam, Co-op & Our Opportunity (2024–2026)

_Synthesized by orchestrator from market sub-research (genre saturation, Steam economics, co-op
virality & flop risks)._

---

## 1. The opportunity, in one paragraph

Co-op PvE is the **best-monetizing pattern on Steam right now** — games with co-op generated
**$4.1B in H1 2025** (+11% YoY, a record), and **all of Steam's top-5 new sellers in 2025 were
co-op.** PvE extraction sells massively even without PvP (Escape from Duckov: 3M copies). And
**sci-fi/space co-op is the explicitly named underserved "2026 trend."** Our concept sits at the
intersection of all three. The catch: discoverability, not demand, is the bottleneck — and the
comedy-horror *facility* corner is saturated, so we must own the **systemic-ship + roguelite +
space-salvage** white space with one legible, clippable hook.

## 1b. The canonical proof points ("cheap and fast wins")

- **PEAK** (2025, Aggro Crab + Landfall): built in **~4 months for under $200k** → **10M+ copies**,
  2M in 9 days. The single clearest evidence that a tiny budget + fast build + one fresh co-op hook
  is the winning template.
- **Schedule I** (2025, *solo* dev): ~8M+ copies, **459,075 peak CCU**, $14.99 — a one-person EA
  game outselling most AAA.
- **Lethal Company** (solo), **Stardew Valley** (solo), **Vampire Survivors** (solo→tiny): the
  format repeatedly rewards micro-teams who nail fun-first over fidelity.

**Implication:** target a PEAK-style envelope — small scope, fast core build, $12.99–$14.99 EA,
"friendslop" production values (rough/stylized is a *feature* that buys speed and reads as charming).
Even the pessimistic scenario roughly breaks even at that budget; the upside is uncapped.

the **differentiators won**. Don't ship "Lethal Company in space" — ship "the Barotrauma-systemic
salvage roguelite" using the proven extraction loop as the chassis.

## 4. Economics & revenue math

- **Steam cut:** 30% up to $10M lifetime gross (25% to $50M, 20% above). A first indie pays 30%.
- **Real net ≈ gross × ~0.52** after Steam (×0.70) + refunds (~12%, ×0.88) + VAT/regional blend
  (×~0.85). Use ~0.60 for US-heavy sales. (Net is NOT ×0.70 — VAT + regional pricing is the most
  underestimated leak.)
- **Refunds:** median ~9.5%; EA ~12.4%; budget 8–12% for a polished launch.
- **The grim median:** 67% of Steam games earn <$5k lifetime; conditioned on price, median ≈ $4k
  (>$5 games) and ≈ $17k (>$10 games). The distribution is brutally top-heavy.

**Scenarios for a polished $12–15 co-op title (year 1):**

| Scenario | WL at launch | Units | Net (~×0.52) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pessimistic | ~3–5k | 3,000–5,000 | ~$19k–50k |
| Base | ~10–35k | 25,000–30,000 | ~$156k–300k |
| Optimistic | ~30k+ | 150,000+ | ~$900k–1.5M+ |
| Tail (unplannable) | viral | 1M+ | $10M+ |

The single biggest lever between pessimistic and base is **wishlist velocity before launch**, not
price or polish.

## 5. Wishlists & discoverability mechanics

- **Popular Upcoming** triggers around **~7,000 wishlists in a compressed window** (velocity > total)
  → front-page visibility snowball. **~10k WL** is the informal comfort floor (Zukowski rule).
- **Launch visibility round:** a one-time impression burst in week ~1–2, scaled to WL/sales velocity.
- Steam emails wishlisters on launch, EA→1.0, demo release, and any discount ≥20% — so a **≥20%
  launch discount** both converts and fires the blast.
- **Caveat — conversion has deflated:** industry-wide wishlist→sale has fallen to **~5–10%** (from
  the legacy ~20% of 2018); model conservatively. First-year cumulative ≈ 15–25% of wishlists.
- **WL→week-1 conversion:** ~15% (<5k WL) rising to ~25% (100k+). **But co-op wildly over-converts**
  — R.E.P.O. ~68×, Webfishing ~67×, Mage Arena ~78×, PEAK ~266× wishlist-to-sales. Our sub-genre is
  one of the strongest-converting on Steam.
- **Next Fest:** you get ONE per game. Feb 2026 median demo added ~806 WL (95th pct ~13,461). Enter
  with >1k WL (below that, median gain ~462); ship the demo *weeks before* the fest for velocity signal.

## 6. Virality factors — why co-op horror explodes

- **Proximity voice chat** is the common thread of every breakout — it manufactures clippable
  emergent comedy/horror (friends separated, screaming, dying mid-sentence, betrayal).
- **Clippable failure moments** + **very low barrier** (cheap, drop-in, learn-in-2-minutes).
- **The "watch-one-streamer → buy-with-friends" loop:** viewers buy a *cluster* of copies to play
  with their own friends — amplifying sales-per-impression.
- **In-game capture mechanics** (Content Warning's SpookTube) make sessions produce ready-to-post
  footage — designed for the flywheel.
- Hard data: **~41 hours watched ≈ 1 copy sold**; multiplayer shows the strongest stream→wishlist
  correlation. The genre is *streamed into existence*, not advertised.

## 7. Risks & the graveyard

- **Empty-lobby / cold-start = the #1 killer.** Typical multiplayer game settles at ~6% of peak
  launch CCU. **Co-op survives because its "atomic network" is just a few friends** (the structural
  reason breakouts are co-op, not PvP). PvP/matchmaking indies almost always die (Rumbleverse ~6mo,
  The Day Before, Disintegration). → **We must have a solo/AI fallback so it's always playable.**
- **No wishlists / no marketing** → thin launch can't produce the simultaneous population co-op needs.
- **No hook / undifferentiated clone** → blends into the dead 79%.
- **Live-service burden** (anti-cheat, servers, balance) is open-ended for a tiny team → favor
  P2P/listen-server + premium model, avoid F2P infra.
- **Trend-timing:** demand is still growing (extraction/co-op horror = fastest-growing indie horror
  category), but the easy-virality window is crowding — differentiation + polish are decisive.

## 8. Top takeaways for SALVAGE

1. Co-op PvE is the strongest-monetizing lane on Steam; we're squarely in it.
2. Own the **systemic-ship + roguelite + space-salvage** white space — not a facility reskin.
3. **Proximity voice + one clippable signature hook** (sabotage/vacuum physics) are non-negotiable.
4. **Solo viability** widens the market beyond friend-groups and fixes the lineage's biggest gap.
5. Premium **$14.99 → $19.99**, clean monetization, cosmetics only later.
6. **Wishlist velocity** (early page + demo + one Next Fest) is the make-or-break lever; target 20–40k.
7. Budget **net ≈ gross × ~0.52**; break-even ~2–6k copies; base case ~$150–300k net; right-skewed upside.
8. Build the **solo/AI fallback** to beat the cold-start problem that kills multiplayer indies.
